# The AI Your Network Can't See: Getting Control of Local AI Models on Employee Laptops

![The AI Your Network Can't See: Getting Control of Local AI Models on Employee Laptops]({{ site.baseurl }}/images/The-AI-Network-Iceberg-Infographic-1536x864.png)

Employees increasingly use local AI models on their laptops to summarize documents, write code, and automate tasks without relying on cloud-based services. While these tools offer greater privacy and flexibility, they also create security risks that traditional network monitoring and cloud security solutions cannot easily detect. Unmanaged AI runtimes may expose sensitive information, operate without authentication, contain unpatched vulnerabilities, or give AI agents unauthorized access to files, credentials, and system commands. As shadow AI adoption grows, organizations risk losing visibility into how confidential business data is accessed, processed, and stored. A modern security strategy requires endpoint-level AI discovery, software version monitoring, vulnerability management, risk assessment, and data protection. Businesses should evaluate AI tools based on their capabilities, permissions, network access, and ability to execute actions rather than simply identifying their names. Instead of banning local AI, organizations should establish approved usage policies and implement endpoint-level controls that allow employees to innovate securely.
