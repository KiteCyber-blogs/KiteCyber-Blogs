# Pinned on Paper, Drifting on the Laptop: What Your MCP Config Doesn't Tell You

![Pinned on Paper, Drifting on the Laptop: What Your MCP Config Doesn't Tell You]({{ site.baseurl }}/images/Pinned-on-Paper-Drifting-on-the-Laptop-1536x864.png)

AI agents and Model Context Protocol (MCP) servers are becoming essential parts of modern development workflows, but unmanaged dependency changes can introduce serious security risks. Even when an MCP server configuration remains unchanged, its dependencies may update automatically, potentially introducing vulnerable or malicious code. Research by Corelight found that one MCP server ran with 15 different dependency sets over five weeks, highlighting how frequently software environments can change without security teams noticing. These servers may also hold sensitive credentials and connect to critical management systems, making dependency drift a potential entry point for attackers. Recent credential-stealing campaigns and research into compromised AI models further demonstrate the growing risks associated with AI toolchains. Organizations need endpoint-level visibility into running processes, package changes, credential access, and network connections rather than relying only on configuration reviews. Kitecyber addresses these challenges through endpoint monitoring that tracks MCP servers, AI agents, device activity, file access, and network behaviour. Its context-aware security controls help identify unexpected changes, monitor sensitive data movement, and prevent unauthorized access. Ultimately, securing MCP servers requires more than approving configurations; businesses must continuously monitor what actually runs on employee devices.
